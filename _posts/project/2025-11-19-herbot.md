Herbot is an autonomous herb management robot I developed as part of the "AI Embedded Programming" course. The system goes beyond simple watering and lighting control—it automatically identifies and removes diseased leaves through vision-based AI.

## Mechanical Design

The robot's manipulation system consists of four motors working in coordination:

- **Servo Motor**: Controls the gripper mechanism for cutting herbs
- **NEMA 17 Stepper**: Drives Z-axis movement via belt-driven conveyor system
- **Linear Actuator**: Provides X-axis (radial) motion for reach
- **DC Motor**: Rotates the plant pot, enabling 360° coverage with a single-rail structure

This design allows full spatial access to all parts of the plant without requiring a complex multi-axis gantry system.

The system runs on three voltage levels: 12V for the stepper motor, 6V for the linear actuator and DC motor, and 5V for the Raspberry Pi. Power distribution is managed through WAGO terminal blocks for clean, modular connections.

## Edge TPU Optimization

To achieve real-time performance on embedded hardware:

| Optimization | Effect |
|--------------|--------|
| INT8 Quantization | Reduced model size, faster inference |
| Edge TPU Compilation | Hardware-accelerated inference |
| No normalization | Direct uint8 input for speed |

**Performance Improvement:**

| Model | CPU | Edge TPU | Speedup |
|-------|-----|----------|---------|
| Herbify | 80-150ms | 10-15ms | ~10x |
| PlantDoc | 70-120ms | 5-12ms | ~10x |

Total inference time: **< 30ms** for both species ID and disease detection.

## Limitations and Lessons

The main constraints were budget and time. Department support covered part of the cost, but Herbot has no proper housing: the base and rotary stage are improvised from a small box, and the camera and gripper are attached to the carriage with adhesive and tape. The build is crude, but it was the cheapest configuration that worked.

The project was a useful exercise in engineering under constraints. Most coursework imposes neither a cost nor a compute budget; Herbot imposed both and forced explicit trade-offs.

The same inexperience with practical constraints is why the first version of Soltrone did not reach a usable state: a flight controller, four propellers, and a Raspberry Pi do not make an autonomous drone without careful attention to power, wiring, and testing. Those lessons carried over to [Soltrone v2]({% post_url project/2026-01-10-soltrone_v2 %}).
